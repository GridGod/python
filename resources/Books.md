# Books and Reading Notes
Complete books, authors and learning resources
I recommend having a small, carefully selected library rather than buying ten books and trying to study all of them simultaneously.
A. Books — your programming library










1. Python Crash Course
- Author: Eric Matthes
- Edition: 3rd Edition
- Publication: 2022
- Publisher: No Starch Press
- Level: Beginner to intermediate
Purpose: Your primary Python textbook. Covers fundamentals, testing, projects and application development.

Priority: Essential
Official book and contents












2. Automate the Boring Stuff with Python
- Author: Al Sweigart
- Edition: 3rd Edition
- Publication: 2025
- Publisher: No Starch Press
- Level: Beginner to intermediate
Purpose: Practical programming, file operations, automation, web scraping and databases.

Priority: Essential
Official book and contents












3. Grokking Algorithms
- Author: Aditya Bhargava
- Edition: 2nd Edition
- Publication: 2024
- Publisher: Manning
- Level: Beginner to intermediate
Purpose: Algorithms, searching, sorting, recursion, graphs and problem-solving.

Priority: Essential for DSA
Publisher's book page












4. Clean Code
- Author: Robert C. Martin
- Edition: 1st Edition
- Publication: 2008
- Publisher: Prentice Hall
Purpose: Professional coding practices, naming, functions, maintainability and refactoring.

Priority: Read selectively after Day 15
Publisher's book page












5. Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach
- Authors: Stuart Russell and Peter Norvig
- Edition: 4th Edition
- Publication: 2020
- Publisher: Pearson
Purpose: A deeper understanding of AI, intelligent agents, search, reasoning and machine learning.

Priority: Future AI studies, not required in 45 days
Official companion website


My purchasing recommendation: Start with Python Crash Course and use Automate the Boring Stuff as your practical reference. You can use the latter's official online edition for free. Do not purchase all five books immediately.
